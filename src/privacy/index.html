Privacy

What this site keeps about you, who can see it, and how to take it away. In plain words, because there is not much of it.

Without an account

You can play every published tune without signing in. Your progress on a tune — the pitches you have written, what each check said, how many checks you made, how long the tab was open — is kept in your own browser, under its local storage, and is never sent to this site. The one thing the server sees is each check you make: the pitches you submit, graded and answered, and not recorded.

With an account

Signing in uses your Google account. Google tells this site three things: a permanent identifier for your Google account (the subject, a number that means nothing outside Google), your email address, and that Google has verified it. The identifier is how the site recognises you next time; the email is kept so that a person reading the database can tell who is who, and is refreshed each time you sign in. No other Google data is requested, and nothing from Google runs on these pages.

Beyond that, an account holds:

  • A username. One is picked for you when the account is made — two plain words — and you can change it on your account page. It is shown beside every tune you publish, unless you choose to be shown as Anonymous, in which case it is kept to yourself.
  • Your tunes, drafts and published. Drafts are yours alone. Published tunes are public: their title, subtitle, instructions, difficulty, the video they point at, and your username (or Anonymous) can be seen by anyone.
  • Your progress on each tune, the same record the browser kept before, filed under your account so it follows you between browsers. Nobody else can see it.
  • Two settings: whether to be shown as Anonymous, and whether your play may count towards the public figures described below.

When you sign in with an account that is new to a browser, the site offers to bring that browser's unsigned-in progress into the account. Nothing moves unless you say yes.

Cookies

Two, both the site's own, neither for tracking. session is what keeps you signed in: a random token that lasts thirty days, extended while you keep visiting, and ended by signing out. The server stores only a hash of it. signin exists for the ten minutes it takes to go to Google and come back, and for nothing else. There are no analytics cookies and no advertising.

YouTube

Tunes are transcriptions of YouTube videos. The page where a tune is played embeds YouTube's own player, and the list of tunes shows thumbnails fetched from YouTube. Those requests go to YouTube, which sees them as it sees any visit to a YouTube page, under Google's privacy policy. This site sends nothing to YouTube beyond what the player and the pictures need.

Public figures

Each tune shows a difficulty, blended from the author's own rating and the ratings of players who solved it — one vote each, with nobody named. It also shows how many solvers gave it a heart, how many players have solved it, and — once at least three players' solves are in — the median completion time, overall and for flawless runs. All of it is aggregate, with nobody named; the author's own playthroughs of their tune are left out of the play figures entirely. The about page explains how the figures work.

Your account page has a switch, “Count my play in public statistics”, that keeps you out of all of it: while it is off you are not asked to rate or upvote, and your ratings, hearts and playthroughs — past ones included — are left out of every figure. The figures are worked out fresh from the stored rows each time they are shown, so turning the switch off takes effect at once, and nothing derived from your play is kept anywhere.

Who can see what

Published tunes and their bylines are public. Drafts, progress, email addresses and the Google identifier are not shown to anyone. The person who runs this site can read its database, and does so to keep it working; the database lives on Cloudflare's infrastructure, which processes it under Cloudflare's privacy policy. No data is sold or shared with anyone else.

Deleting your account

Your account page has a button for it. Deleting your account removes everything: every tune you published (and, with them, other players' progress on them), your drafts, your progress, your settings, your username, your email and the Google identifier. It cannot be undone. If you want a tune kept, it is yours to leave up by keeping the account.
