tune up

About

What this site is, and how some of its numbers are arrived at.

This site

tune up is a small site for writing down music by ear: somebody transcribes a passage from a video and publishes it as a tune, and everybody else tries to hear it note by note.

The difficulty rating

Every tune shows its difficulty as five chili peppers, from half a pepper to five. The figure starts as the author's own rating, which counts as if a few players had voted it. Each player who solves the tune can then rate it, one vote each, changeable at any time — and the figure shown is the average of all the votes, rounded to the nearest half.

Whose votes count is decided fresh every time the figure is read, never stored. If you turn off “Count my play in public statistics” on your account page, your ratings leave every figure at once — past ratings included — and deleting your account does the same. Unpublishing a tune erases all of its ratings: if it is published again, it starts over from the author's word, since the music may have changed.

Hearts and play figures

A solver who liked a tune can give it a heart, once, and take it back by pressing again; the card shows how many hearts a tune holds. Beside the hearts, each tune counts the players who have solved it, and its box shows the median completion time — overall, and for flawless runs (solved on the first check). A median appears only once at least three players' solves are behind it; before that, the box shows a dash rather than publishing one or two players' own times. The author's playthroughs of their own tune never count in the play figures, and everything above obeys the same switch as the ratings: turn off “Count my play in public statistics” and your hearts and playthroughs leave every figure at once.
